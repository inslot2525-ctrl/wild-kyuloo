# Demo video: voiceover script

About 2 minutes 40 seconds (roughly 360 spoken words at a calm pace). Check the Devpost rules for the length limit before final export, and cut the optional lines (marked *optional*) if you need to.

**Before recording**
- Record the **live site** (https://crowd-kit.onrender.com) after Om's deploy, or `localhost` with real keys. Never demo mode: it shows "Sample" badges.
- Run the Eminem preset and the `/crowds` comparison once beforehand so they replay from the cache. Show a few seconds of the live trace, then cut the wait in editing.
- Close other tabs. Make sure no keys, emails or terminal windows are on screen.
- Browser at 100% zoom, 1920×1080. Record the voice separately and lay it over the screen recording.

---

## 1. The problem (0:00–0:15)

**Screen:** the landing page, slow scroll over the form.

> Every event organiser makes the same bets. Which food stalls to book. Which sponsors to pitch. What merch to print. How many volunteers on the door. Most of those bets are gut feel, and getting them wrong costs money, and sometimes safety.

## 2. The idea (0:15–0:30)

**Screen:** fill in or pick the **Eminem at The Fillmore** preset. Click **Find on Qloo**; the artist card appears.

> Crowd Kit plans your event around the crowd that's actually coming. Tell it the venue, the act, the date, and how many people and volunteers you have. First, it confirms the act on Qloo, so every answer is about the right artist.

## 3. The agent at work (0:30–0:50)

**Screen:** click **Plan my event**. Show the live trace for a few seconds: Qloo calls with receipt numbers, the map lookup, weather. Cut to the finished plan.

> Then an AI agent goes to work. It asks Qloo who Eminem's fans are, what they eat, which brands they love, and how they differ from the venue's usual crowd. At the same time it maps the venue: entrances, transit, food nearby and the weather.

## 4. The plan (0:50–1:20)

**Screen:** scroll through the plan: Who's coming, Food & drink, Sponsors with pitch, Volunteers. Hover a **Qloo** badge and open a receipt.

> Here's the plan. Six food stalls for six slots, with real nearby vendors ranked for this crowd. Sponsors whose fans are already in the room, each with a pitch ready to send. Volunteers by station, from a fixed formula, and even the weights come from Qloo's age profile of this crowd.
>
> Every line shows where it came from. Click a Qloo badge, and you see the exact Qloo call behind it.

## 5. Why Qloo matters (1:20–1:45)

**Screen:** the **With vs without Qloo** tab, then the `/evidence` page.

> Here's the same model with the same brief, and no taste data. Without Qloo, you get about eleven items, every one a guess. With Qloo, over thirty, and nine in ten tied to real data. We measured this across eight real events in the US, UK and India: on average, sixty-one percent of every plan comes straight from Qloo.

## 6. Same venue, different crowd (1:45–2:10)

**Screen:** `/crowds` with Eminem vs Phoebe Bridgers. Show "What changes on the night", the two age charts, then sponsors side by side.

> Here's what taste data really changes. Same venue, same night, same headcount. Only the act changes. Eminem's crowd leans male, Phoebe Bridgers' crowd is strongly female and much younger. So the sponsors change, from Nike and Beats to Polaroid and Patagonia. The merch, the food, the playlist and the merch team change too. Every difference here is computed from Qloo data.

## 7. Safety on the site map (2:10–2:30)

**Screen:** `/map`. Drop or drag a pin, set an exit width, show the safety checks updating. *Optional:* run the AI layout review.

> Finally, the site map. Place the stage, exits, stalls and first aid on a real map. Crowd Kit checks the layout live: how crowded the standing area is, with a crush warning, and how long it takes to get everyone out, even with the biggest exit blocked.

## 8. Close (2:30–2:40)

**Screen:** back to the finished plan, or the landing page with the logo.

> Crowd Kit. Plan for the crowd that's coming, not the crowd you imagine.
